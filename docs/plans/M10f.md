**Sources:** `docs/plans/M10-orchestration.md` (the orchestration ADR: the M10f row of Order, Point 9

choices", empty states, §5 postures); LOCAL-FIRST-AND-DEVICE-MESH.

## What is built

1. **Device roles** (`purposes` in code, "Roles" on screen), chosen at pairing, confirmed on the hub's
   approval screen, stored on the device record, editable in Settings › Devices, tiered as the ADR says.
2. **The placement resolver** (`src/core/services/placement.ts`): pinned, preferred plus failover, and
   floating, resolved against roles, hardware and health. Pure, with a test per mode and per refusal.
   Nothing deploys to other devices yet (M10h); the resolver is called at proposal (the Service
   Manager, and a tier-0 dry run on the hub), and the device agent checks again.
3. **Onboarding, supervisor only:** setup's conversation gains the ADR's order (detect the device,
   create or join a network with this hub's roles, the backend for this OS) and Amendment B Part 2
   (discovery, a consent-first import offer, a populated first view with one suggested action),
   ending in the existing first task.
4. **Settings › Services, minimal:** the backend in use and what it means on this OS, where the switch
   will be, and that it isn't built yet.
5. **The fresh-user test, timed:** a new user reaches a first useful outcome in under 15 minutes of
   setup, measured in the test, with the path recorded as evidence.

## 1. Device roles

- **The six:** `thin-client` (Thin client: a screen onto Nova), `hub-candidate` (Hub candidate: could
  become the hub; its key is pinned), `compute`, `storage`, `capture-source`, `general-server`. The
  field is `purposes`, because `role` is already the mesh role (client, spoke, standby, hub).
- **What each kind may hold:** a phone or browser runs no device agent, so it is a thin client and only
  that. A computer may hold any of the six. A standby always holds `hub-candidate` (it is one: it can
  take over), plus workload roles if the person wants. The hub always holds `hub-candidate` and
  `general-server` (it runs Nova and its services); compute, storage and capture source are the
  person's choice.
- **Combined with hardware:** roles are labels. Hardware stays where it is (`device_hardware`, from
  the heartbeat). The resolver reads both.
- **At pairing:** the device says what it is for (`claims.purposes`); `/pair` says plainly that a
  phone or browser is a thin client; the computer's command takes `--roles compute,storage`. The hub's
  approval sheet shows them as a multi-select of what that kind may hold, pre-set to what was asked,
  and the person confirms or changes them there.
- **Tiers** (the ADR's table): pairing is tier 3 already. **Widening** a device's roles is tier 3
  (`confirm: true`). **Narrowing** is tier 2 (`approved: true`), and Nova says which services it
  would stop there (today: none, since nothing runs on other devices before M10h).
- **Hub candidate is itself tier 3, every time** (Decision 2): at pairing and in Settings, adding it
  to a computer needs its own `confirm_candidate: true`, its own checkbox on the sheet, saying its key
  is pinned so it could become the hub. A standby's tier-3 approval is its candidacy (the ADR: its key
  is pinned through its tier-3 standby enrollment). **Pinned in the next roster:** the signed roster
  gains `candidates` (each candidate's id, address and keys) beside `hub` and `standby`. Removing the
  role drops it from the next roster.
- **The hub stays elected.** A candidacy only makes a device eligible; promotion is M10g.
- **Devices paired before this** (migration 0019): phones and browsers become thin clients; the
  standby becomes a hub candidate with no new confirmation; computers get no workload role; the hub
  gets `hub-candidate` and `general-server`.

## 2. The placement resolver

`resolvePlacement(def, devices, { now, failoverAfterMs, current })`, pure. Each device is judged
**eligible or not, with the reason in words**, then the mode picks:
- **Eligible** means: trusted; not a phone or browser; holding a workload role (a thin-client-only or
  role-less device never receives a workload); holding every role the definition asks for
  (`placement.roles`, default `general-server`); its hardware meeting `needs` (`gpu_vram_gb`,
  `memory_gb`, `disk_gb`; Apple's unified memory counts as the GPU's) and `resources.memory_mb`, with
  no hardware report meaning "not known yet", never "fits"; a program for its platform, or Linux for
  an image-only service.
- **Pinned:** that device only. If it isn't eligible, refused with its reason. If it is down (not
  heard from within the Online window), the service is down and the resolution says so.
- **Preferred plus failover:** the best eligible, healthy device (most GPU memory when GPU memory is
  needed, else most memory). A service already placed stays while its device is healthy, or unhealthy
  for less than the failover window. Past the window, it moves to the failover target: a named
  eligible device, or `any` (the next best).
- **Floating:** stateless only (the validator already refuses state); one instance on each of up to
  `replicas` healthy eligible devices, the best first, saying when fewer are eligible.

**Checked twice** (Point 9): at proposal, by the Service Manager (`placement` option) and the hub's
dry run `POST /services/placement` (tier 0: drafting), which refuse a placement no device may take,
naming each device's reason; and by the device agent, `assignmentAllowed(purposes, workload)` in
`src/device/trust.ts`, which refuses an assignment its own stored roles don't allow. The boot agent
keeps its roles in `device.json`, from the approval and from each heartbeat answer the hub sends over
its own session. **Nothing deploys to other devices yet:** a resolution to a device that isn't the
hub is refused by the supervisor driver with "native workloads come with M10h", as in M10e.

## 3. Onboarding

**The flow, in the ADR's order, then Amendment B Part 2, then what setup already asked:**

| Step | Nova says | The answer |
|---|---|---|
| `device` | What it detected here: the OS (Linux, macOS, Windows, or Linux inside WSL2), CPU, memory, GPUs, free disk. It proposes a new Nova network with this computer as its hub. "Join" explains that this computer then joins as a device of the existing hub (Settings › Devices › Add a device, then the command), and stays on this step | "new" (recommended) or "join" |
| `roles` | What this hub is for, besides being the hub, with Nova's recommendation from the hardware (general server always; compute when a GPU has 8 GB or more) | a multi-select; "yes" keeps the recommendation |
| `backend` | The per-OS summary (below), the supervisor pre-selected, that it can be changed later in Settings › Services | one option, pre-selected; K3s shown as not available yet on Linux |
| `user_name` … `privacy` | Unchanged (name, timezone, Nova's name, directness, privacy) | as before |
| `discover` | Whether to look for devices and services: on this computer, or on the network too. Looking finds; it connects to nothing. On the network it sends a short query to the devices there and nothing about the person, and under local-only it says that this goes beyond this machine | this computer / network too / no |
| `import` | What it found on this computer that it could bring in (notes, calendar, bookmarks), by place and count of files, nothing read yet; each item is checked as outside content and then waits in Memory for the person's OK | which, all, or none. Skipped (with the import folder named) when nothing was found or discovery was declined |
| `first_task` | Unchanged: the welcome note, checked | yes / no |
| done | **The first view** | — |

**The per-OS summaries, worded to the evidence** (the closing rule; the ADR's draft wording names
things M10h and M10k build, so it is cut to what is true now):
- **Windows and macOS:** Nova runs its services as ordinary programs under its supervisor. Everything
  Nova does today runs that way, and nothing in Nova needs Kubernetes (the M10e suite, run with a
  tripwire on every Kubernetes tool). K3s isn't offered: it needs Linux. What this computer can't do
  is host container services; those would run on a Linux device. You can see this in Settings ›
  Services, where the switch will be.
- **Linux inside WSL2:** counts as Windows here (K3s inside WSL2 is out of scope for v1), said so.
- **Linux:** the supervisor, recommended and pre-selected: lighter, nothing extra to install. K3s is
  shown as **not available yet** in this version, with what it would add. It is never a working claim
  and never a quiz.

**The first view** (`view.first`, at the end of setup, never a blank chat alone): what was found (each
"found, not connected"), what was imported (waiting in Memory), this device's roles and backend, and
**one** suggested first action. The action is picked from a short list of intents, each carrying the
eval tags that back it, and only an intent whose tags the regression suite demonstrates can be
offered (`tests/core/onboard.test.ts` checks every intent against `evals.demonstrated(TASKS)`):
- "Review what Nova brought in" (Memory's waiting list) when anything was imported: `import`,
  `memory-review`;
- otherwise "Bring in your notes" (the import folder, `~/workspace/import/`): `first-run`, `import`.

**Empty states** follow one pattern: orient, state the value, offer exactly one action. The ones on
the first session's path are changed (Home's empty conversation, Memory with nothing kept, Tasks with
nothing, Devices with only this one), with a test that each has one action. Others are listed as a
limit, not changed, to keep out of the parallel copy and posture work.

## 4. Settings › Services (minimal)

Settings gains a Services category with one section: the backend in use ("Ordinary programs, under
Nova's supervisor"), the same per-OS words, and "Switching to K3s isn't available yet. When it is, it
will be here, and it asks for your explicit confirmation (tier 3)." There is no switch to press,
because there is nothing to switch to. The map and what runs where are M10i's.

## 5. The timed first win

The fresh-user test (`tests/acceptance/fresh-user.test.ts`) keeps every check it had and gains:
- the device steps, discovery against fake responders on 127.0.0.1 (a stand-in Ollama on HTTP and an
  mDNS responder answering for a placeholder Home Assistant, reached by unicast to loopback), and an
  import of notes, bookmarks and a calendar file from the test's blank user folder;
- **the first win:** from the first `GET /setup` to the person keeping one imported note in Memory and
  Nova finding it by a word from it (`/api/v1/memory/search`). The clock runs over the whole path; the
  test fails at 15 minutes. Each step's time is written into the evidence (`NOVA_EVIDENCE`).
- **Said plainly:** the test answers at machine speed, so its measured time is the system's time, not
  a person's. The evidence also states the human path: the number of answers and of words Nova shows,
  with a stated reading and answering pace, as an estimate.

A browser run of the same path (`tests/ui/m10f-onboarding.test.ts`) times it in Chromium, and shows
the per-OS summaries for stand-in Linux, macOS, Windows and WSL2 detections.

## Stand-ins and limits during the build

- The OS detection is a stand-in in tests: `NOVA_DETECT_OS=linux|macos|windows|wsl2` (only the words
  change; nothing else reads it).
- Discovery in tests: `NOVA_DISCOVERY_PORTS` and `NOVA_DISCOVERY_MDNS=127.0.0.1:<port>` point it at
  fake responders on loopback. No test sends multicast or touches the real network; a test checks the
  discovery socket was bound to 127.0.0.1. The real multicast path (224.0.0.251:5353) is the same codec
  and is not run in tests (a limit).
- No real device is paired, no workload is placed on a spoke, and no real network is scanned.

## Gate

The ADR's gates for M10f (Points 5, 9 and 11), Amendment B Part 2's, and the closing rule:
1. Pairing with roles in the browser test; the approval screen shows and can change them.
2. Placement on a thin client is refused at proposal and again at the device.
3. An edit in Settings › Devices is tiered: widening tier 3, narrowing tier 2, saying what it would stop.
4. Making a device a hub candidate asks at tier 3, at pairing and later, and pins its key in the next
   roster (Decision 2).
5. Resolver tests, one per mode and per refusal; a preferred service moves to its failover when the
   stand-in device stops heartbeating past the window.
6. Browser tests of the flow on stand-in Linux, macOS, Windows and WSL2 detections, each with its own
   summary; the supervisor pre-selected; K3s never offered as working.
7. Settings › Services exists and says where the switch will be (the tier-3 switch itself is M10k's).
8. Discovery against fake loopback responders, found and not connected; import consent-first through
   the ingest gate; the populated first view with one action; empty states with one action.
9. The fresh-user test passes, with the timed first win under 15 minutes and its path as evidence.
10. Every new onboarding claim is in the claims register, backed by regression tasks or worded as a
    fact about the configuration.
11. `npm run check` and `scripts/ui-test.sh` (Chromium and WebKit) pass.

## Not in M10f

Hub promotion and fencing over every candidate (M10g: the roster lists candidates now, and device
agents keep pinning only the hub and the standby until fencing covers candidates); native workloads
and any deploy to a spoke (M10h); the map (M10i); the K3s driver and K3s in onboarding (M10j/M10k);
the tier-3 backend switch (M10k); one installer for every platform and a PowerShell front door (signed
packages are the owner's call); a real LAN discovery run.

## For the coordinator: the plain-language "What changed" entry (not written to the LEDGER here)

**M10f · Devices say what they're for, and setup shows what Nova found**
- What changed: when you add a device, you now say what it's for (a screen only, a possible future
  hub, running models, storage, capture, other services), and you confirm it on the hub. A device that's
  only a screen never gets anything to run. Setup now starts with this computer: what it is, that it's
  your hub, and how Nova runs its services here. Then, if you say so, it looks for things on this
  computer and your network (finding, not connecting), offers to bring in your notes, bookmarks and
  calendar (each checked first, then waiting for your OK), and ends on a page showing what it found and
  one thing to do next.
- What you can now do: pick a device's roles when you add it, and change them in Settings › Devices
  (adding asks for your explicit confirmation; removing asks first). See how Nova runs services on
  this computer in Settings › Services.
- Still unfinished: nothing runs on other devices yet, and Kubernetes isn't available. Making a device
  the hub is a later step. Looking on your network hasn't been tried on a real network yet.
