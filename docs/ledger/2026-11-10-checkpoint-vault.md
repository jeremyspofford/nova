**2026-11-10 · Checkpoints · The vault is never put back by a restore (F-014)**
- What changed: deleting a secret rotates the vault's key, but checkpoints copied the whole vault (key and secrets), and restoring one put back the secrets "as they were then", so a deleted secret and the old key came back. Checkpoints now leave the vault out (the manifest says why, as for the broker), and a checkpoint restore always carries the vault as it is now, the way it already carries this node's keys. That also covers checkpoints taken before this change, which still hold the old vault.
- What you can now do: delete a secret and know no restore brings it back.
- Still unfinished: a vault damaged after a checkpoint can't be repaired by restoring it; deleting and re-adding the affected secrets is the way. If the owner wants checkpoints to repair a vault, that needs a design that keeps the deletion guarantee (the PR says so). Old checkpoints keep their vault copy on disk until retention removes them.
- Checked: the restore round trip now deletes a secret after the checkpoint and adds another. After the restore the deleted one stays gone, the other two are there and the key is now's. A checkpoint's manifest lists no vault files (that assertion fails on the old code). Checkpoint tests, typecheck and the full suite pass.
