**2026-11-06 · Models · Wake-on-LAN only on the device's own network, opt-in, rate-limited, logged**
- What changed: Nova now sends a wake signal only onto the network the sleeping computer is on, from the hub's own network card on that network, to that card's broadcast address. Before, the hub sent from whatever card the operating system picked, to any private address, and fell back to 255.255.255.255. It also counted the mesh's addresses and very broad netmasks as a network to wake on. Now the hub refuses: a computer on a network the hub isn't on, an address on the mesh (including 100.64.0.0/10), a netmask broader than /16, narrower than /30, or not a proper netmask, and a broadcast address the computer reported itself. There is no 255.255.255.255 fallback any more. A target you set yourself (`PUT /models/wake`) must be on that same network, or that computer's wake is refused. Waking is now your choice for each computer ("Nova wakes it" or "Wait until it's turned on", `PUT /models/devices/{id}/power`); it is off until you turn it on. On the first run after this update, a computer that serves a model role and that Nova has woken before (the Dell) keeps waking on. Nova sends at most 4 wakes per computer and 12 across the hub per hour, and one automatic wake per task. After a wake that didn't work, Nova stops waking that computer by itself until it is heard from again. Every wake, sent or refused, is written to the wake table with who asked (routing or you), which card sent it, and why it was refused. It is also recorded in the Work Ledger: as an event on the task it served, or, when you asked, as its own task "Wake ⟨computer⟩" that doesn't ask first (tier 1). The hub now drops network cards with a multicast, broadcast or all-zero MAC. A MAC that two computers both report wakes neither and opens a problem. The wake and hardware tables now copy to a standby, so a hub that takes over can still wake a sleeping computer and keeps counting the limits.
- What you can now do: choose for each computer whether Nova may wake it. Each wake shows on its task, or as its own "Wake ⟨computer⟩" task, with the reason when Nova refused.
- Still unfinished: the sleep policy (Nova putting a computer to sleep after it has been idle), the wake states on the Models screen, the fallbacks after a failed wake, and the device agent reporting whether its card has Wake-on-LAN on are the next PR. The Models screen still shows the old wake sentence for each computer, now with the reason when Nova won't wake it.
- Checked: new tests use a stand-in sender that records each packet's bytes, target, port and bound address and opens no socket. They cover the wake itself (three 102-byte packets to 192.168.1.255:9 from 192.168.1.10) and each refusal with no packet sent: opt-in off, another network, mesh only, a /8 and an empty netmask, bad MACs, Wake-on-LAN off, your target on another network, a shared MAC, the per-computer and hub-wide rate limits, and a second wake for one task. Over HTTP, the console's wake makes a tier-1 task and sends; a paired computer gets 403 and a public caller 401, with nothing sent or recorded. The one real-socket test sends to a loopback listener only. See the PR's gate table.
