Bear Mountain State Park opened in 1913 on the west bank of the Hudson,
created after a public campaign stopped the state from building a prison at
Bear Mountain. It is run by the Palisades Interstate Park Commission.

Perkins Memorial Drive climbs to the 1,289-foot summit; the tower at the top
dates to 1934. The first section of the Appalachian Trail ever built was cut
here in 1923, and the Civilian Conservation Corps did most of the trail and
stonework in the 1930s.

The park holds the Bear Mountain Inn (1915), a swimming pool fed from Hessian
Lake, a merry-go-round with hand-carved native animals, and the
[[Trailside Museums and Zoo]] at the foot of the mountain.

Jeremy goes for the Major Welch trail rather than the road; the summit
parking fills before nine on a summer weekend.
