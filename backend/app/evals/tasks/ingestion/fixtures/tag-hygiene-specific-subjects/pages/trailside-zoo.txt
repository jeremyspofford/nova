Trailside Museums and Zoo
Bear Mountain State Park, Palisades Interstate Park Commission, New York

The Trailside Museums and Zoo opened in 1927 and is the oldest nature-trail
museum complex in the United States. It sits at the base of Bear Mountain,
directly on the Appalachian Trail — the lowest point on the entire 2,197-mile
trail, at 124 feet above sea level, runs through the zoo grounds. Thru-hikers
walk through the exhibits as part of the trail itself.

The complex is four small museums linked by a third of a mile of paved path:
the Reptile House, the Nature Study Museum, the Geology Museum, and the
History Museum, which occupies a 1935 stone building on the site of Fort
Clinton, a Revolutionary War earthwork captured by the British in October
1777.

Animal residents are native New York wildlife that cannot be returned to the
wild — black bears, bald eagles, coyotes, foxes, owls, and porcupines, all
placed here by state wildlife rehabilitators. The zoo does not acquire
animals for display and does not breed.

The site is operated jointly by the Palisades Interstate Park Commission and
the Bear Mountain Trailside Museums and Wildlife Sanctuary, a nonprofit that
funds the animal care program.
